# pilot-02 claim 2: 1.1437 test bpb on 16 seeds, against 1.1555

1. Mechanism. The gain is bytes. I priced a short run of each entry through the real meter. The record costs 14,530,884 ticks per step (3,548 per trained byte) and claim 2 costs 11,083,207 (2,706). On the 4e10 budget that is 2,750 steps and 11.26 M trained bytes against 3,606 steps and 14.77 M, which is 31.1% more and 22% of train.bin. Step counts derived from CI ticks and from CI python line counts agree for all five entries. The fold saves 2.35 M ticks per step (16.1%: cross entropy 1.55 M, matmuls 0.78 M). The schedule saves 1.10 M (7.6%). Together they save 3.45 M (23.7%), an exact sum, because they touch different ops.

The ablations add up. Against the record's 1.15664 on dev, fold_only reads 1.14817, a gain of 0.00847 +- 0.00104 on 19.3% more bytes, and lazy_only reads 1.15330, a gain of 0.00333 +- 0.00105 on 8.2% more. The sum is 0.01180, and claim 2 gains 0.01182 +- 0.00092 over 16 test seeds, so there is no interaction. Mixing splits is fair here: the record's dev to test shift is 0.0011 and the claim's, from the agent's 4 dev seeds, is 0.0010. The fold carries 72% and the schedule 28%. The record's budget curve in entries/TUNING.md (1.1955 at 2e10, 1.1320 at 8e10, one seed each, not rerun by me), with a quadratic through those points and 1.15664, gives 0.032 bpb per doubling at 4e10. It predicts 0.00353 for lazy_only, 0.00761 for fold_only and 0.01133 for claim 2, each inside the realized interval. So the split shows one mechanism, not two: claim 2 scores like the record given 5.2e10 ticks, with no new modelling. Staleness has no visible cost at this budget, although orders 8 to 12 update 112 times against 687 and only 20% to 25% of their touches in a 32-batch cycle land on rows hit once.

2. Rare bytes. Honest and nearly free. The list has 100 bytes in 100 distinct classes of 130, so 30 classes are dead at eval. It came from train: the only byte counts in the transcript are two bincounts of data/train.bin (events 121 and 366), the list appears at event 166, and no tool input names heldout.bin. CLASSES and FLOOR were set in that first draft and never swept. It is character ranges plus UTF-8 punctuation bytes, not a fit: 12 candidates never occur in train, 29 never occur in the scored dev windows, and no scored held-out byte is missing from train. It holds at most 243 bits (log2 of 256 choose 100), so it cannot carry held-out windows. The other new literals are EVERY, CLASSES and FLOOR, and the file uses 1,331 of 4,096 literal bytes.

Cost on dev, exact up to the verifier's renormalization because a non-candidate's p does not depend on the params: 2 of 524,288 scored targets (bytes 177 and 195), 23.25 bits each, no vote rescue, 0.000089 bpb. A 256-way model that knew the train frequencies would pay 44.8 bits against these 46.5, a net 0.000003 bpb. FLOOR's mass costs 0.000037 bpb on every byte. The total is about 0.00013, or 1% of the gain. Test has 1 such target (byte 166), 0.000044 bpb. The scored windows are not unusually clean: all held-out windows have 15 of 11,031,040 on dev and 16 of 11,030,528 on test, and train has 326 of 65,995,582. The gain side, whatever mass the record wastes on those 156 slots, cannot be counted without trained params. fold_only's excess over the budget curve is 0.00086, inside its interval, so that gain is about 0.001 at most. Every byte keeps p above zero and the verifier renormalizes, so this is a proper distribution with a fixed escape price that a real coder could use. It only works on a near-ASCII corpus.

3. Price. No exploit. Accumulating is priced: a backward with a gradient waiting pays 1 tick per 4 floats (_engine.py line 217) and every embedding backward pays table.size // 16, so a big table costs 0.375 ticks per float per step at every-32 against 0.8125 at every-4, a 54% saving. tools/calibrate.py never times that branch, since its setup clears grad, so I did, on this Mac under load: 0.34 to 0.48 ns per tick, against 0.41 for the table update, 0.40 for embedding backward and 0.90 for cross entropy forward.

On 2-core Actions runners, train ns per tick is 2.860 for the record (4 dev seeds), 2.823 lazy_only, 2.605 fold_only, 2.274 claim 2. Eval is 3.302, 3.256, 3.184, 2.841. These were not one host: claim 2 and fold_only have the same forward at the same ticks yet differ by a factor of 1.12, and twin noise is exactly 0.0 on the claim jobs and 7e-6 to 2e-5 on the others. Corrected by 1.12, claim 2 trains at 2.55 ns per tick, 11% below the record, and takes 102 s against 114 s. Same-host, lazy_only against the record shows the schedule does not raise ns per tick. Bytes per second rise 47%, more than bytes per tick, so a wall-clock budget would favour the entry more. The cut ops were the dear ones per tick, and the spend moved to embedding backward (45% of ticks against 34%). Eval is 11,458.7 ticks per byte against 11,153.3, 23% of the 50,000 cap. The place loop takes 258 ticks per byte and the entry runs 59,631 eval python lines of 20 M, all paid for: fold_only's eval takes 23.9 s against the record's 24.1 s. This is a reallocation inside the price list.

No shrinkage. The headline should be the 16-seed 1.1437, since stage 1 alone reads 1.1434.
