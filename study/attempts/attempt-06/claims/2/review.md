# Review of attempt-06, claim 2: thinner high-order tables and a cheaper gate step

Numbers come from verify.json, outcome.json, the record's test.json and dev.json, attempt-05's verify.json, the null controls, the agent's transcript and work files, and my own replay and pricing on this Mac. I ran nothing at the real budget and read no held-out byte; my checks use train.bin.

1. Mechanism. The gain is real extra training bytes per tick in both phases, paid for mainly by dropping four in-window stages and by thinner rows, and not by the equality matrix the claim leads with. I replayed train() by pricing each distinct step once and adding that price for its repeats. The totals match the logs exactly: 39,982,688,386 for the record, 39,989,474,907 for claim 1, 39,988,399,260 for claim 2.
Table phase: 2,682 steps and 21,970,944 target bytes at 1,672.8 ticks per byte, against 2,376 steps and 19,464,192 at 1,888.7, so 1.129 times the bytes per tick. Mean step price falls 11.4%, from 15.47M to 13.70M. One high-order table's backward falls from 1,099,575 to 575,287 ticks per 8,192 rows, which is the claim's halving.
Gate phase: 424 steps and 434,176 bytes at 7,446 ticks per byte, against 244 steps and 249,856 at 12,883, so 1.730 times the bytes per tick. Claim 1 has 353 steps. Step price falls from 13,191,922 to 7,625,129.
What buys the gate step, at the four stages it ended on. Forward plus backward is 13.05M for the record and 7.52M for the entry. Dropping four stages with everything else reverted gives 8.20M, so stage removal is 4.85M of the 5.53M fall. Building constants once saves 689k and the reciprocal 359k. Putting per-order hashes back gives 7.15M, which is 370k cheaper than the entry. The unused m16 chain in `local_stats` costs 371k on every step and accounts for all of that. So the matrix was a real saving at eight stages (13.18M to 12.12M in the agent's log, g0 to g1), but at four stages it saves nothing and the entry overpays by 4.9%, about 21 gate steps. The claim's account is wrong on this point. The gain does not depend on it.
The matrix prices what it does. It is not a matmul. The diagonals are slices, which touch no elements for 2,000 ticks, and each product is a full T by T by B elementwise mul at 1 tick per 4 elements. Build is 305,764 ticks and the chain to order 6 is 417,216, at 0.89 and 0.54 ns per tick on this Mac, against 0.25 for the stage matmul.
What each part gains. At full budget on dev seeds 11 and 12: record 1.09394, v5 (thin tables only) 1.09263, x08 (this code) 1.09064, so 0.0013 from thin tables and 0.0020 from the gate changes. Gate sensitivity is clean in the agent's fixed-table runs: 263, 428 and 1055 steps give 1.09255, 1.0907 and 1.08732, or 0.0026 per doubling. Going from 244 to 424 steps is 0.80 doublings and predicts 0.0021, against 0.00215 measured offline (g0 to gw2, seed 11). More gate bytes therefore explain the gate part, and the stage set and the 0.15 rate directly add about nothing within the 0.0005 harness noise. By price shares, stage removal is 0.0017 of that, constants and reciprocal 0.0005, and the matrix gives back 0.0002.
The sealed split agrees. Against all 24 record seeds (1.09278), claim 1 at 353 steps gains 0.0030, of which the slope gives 0.0014, leaving 0.0016 for thin tables. Claim 2 adds 0.00094 against a predicted 0.0007. That is about 0.0016 from tables and 0.0023 from the gate.
The thin-table figure is a net one. From e08 against e13 and x08 against x065, table ticks are worth about 0.017 to 0.027 per doubling, so 12.9% more steps alone should give 0.0030 to 0.0048, and thinner rows appear to hand back 0.0017 to 0.0035. That rests on two-seed runs and is rough. "Reduces collisions" has no separate measurement.

2. Honesty. Clean. forward(params, x) receives only x, since verify passes rows cut to the context and never the targets. There is no state: module level holds constants, window constants are rebuilt per forward call, `static_problems` returns nothing, and sha256 fdac3262 and code hash b180e844 match. The matrix `e` does hold every pair, future ones included. Causality rests on `mask` (tril at -1), which m1 carries into every order and the word stages multiply in, and on `follows[j]` being byte j+1 with j < i, so already seen. On train bytes the stage counts match the record's hash counts cell for cell for orders 3, 6, 101 and 102: 0 of 622,592 cells differ at eval shape and 0 of 77,824 at gate shape. A brute-force count agrees for orders 3 and 6. So predictions are unchanged by the matrix. Replacing all bytes from the cut on, for 13 cuts from 1 to 511 over 16 rows, moves logits before the cut by exactly 0, and so does shuffling the rows. As a control, 185 of 208 rows moved at the cut itself.
Twin values are exactly 0.0 on all 16 seeds. That is a host property and not a feature: the record's own code shows 0.0 on 16 seeds in attempt-05's verify and 8.1e-6 to 1.8e-5 in test.json, and both entries give 0 on this Mac. These features cannot defeat the check, because nothing reads past the position. A bitwise zero also rules out a bonus under the 1e-4 tolerance at the probed positions.

3. Dev tuning. `seed_runs_before` of 46 is exact for verify runs: 26 before claim 1, 8 for claim 1, 4 for x08 and x065, 8 for claim 2. It undercounts all the same. work/gb.py scored 31 offline gate refits on all 1,024 dev windows, every one on seed 11 and v5's cached tables, so there were 77 dev scorings and 40% are unrecorded. No script calls verify's children. The agent built 50 variants and scored 44 (16 by verify, 28 offline), against its "roughly 30". Its other statement holds: the last choices were noise-level. The final seven stage sets span 1.09077 to 1.09139 on one seed, and it took the second lowest. The 0.15 rate moved 0.00053.
The 0.0041 reflects little of that selection. Dev gain is 0.00417 and test gain 0.00409 over 16 seeds. Going from dev to test, claim 2 drops 0.00199 and the record 0.00207, so nothing is lost crossing to the sealed split. On fresh seeds there is no shrinkage either: harness 1.09083, seed 11 at full budget 1.0907, 8 fresh dev seeds 1.09087. The two-seed gain of 0.0033 is smaller only because the record's 1.0939 on seeds 11 and 12 sits 0.0011 under its own 8-seed mean.
Constants carry ordinary tuning and no held-out data. The new numbers are ROWS 203999, width 4 and rate 0.15; the retained LOCAL values are the record's, unchanged. A choice among 44 is about 5.5 bits. No test window was read: gb.py names dev only, no command names the test split, and isolation.json shows 0 nonzero bytes in 21,544 test windows. There were no WebFetch or WebSearch calls and no curl or wget, so no attempt to rebuild the split.

4. Price exploitation. I find no dodge of the meter. Item reads are 0. Python lines are 1,710,565 of 20,000,000 (record 1,452,646), eval lines 103,412. Peak memory is 198 MB against 199. Parameters are 4,156,376 plus 34 name characters against a cap of 4,194,304. Eval is 8,059 ticks per byte against the 50,000 cap and the record's 12,171. Literals are 3,150 of 4,096 bytes and syntax nodes 4,024 of 8,192.
The stage matmul is a cheap way to count votes: 19.8M multiply-adds for 160,064 ticks, where the same work elementwise would list at 31 times that. It is inherited, the dense product really runs, and its dimensions clear the thin-product floors. The entry does 4 per step against 8, and its share of the gate step falls from 9.7% to 8.4%, so it leans on this less than the record. The reciprocal is ordinary arithmetic at the listed prices.
test.json is not a comparable host. The record's code ran 2.869 ns per tick there and 3.92 in eval, but 1.446 to 1.465 and 1.99 to 2.01 in attempt-05's verify. The entry runs 1.724 to 1.754 and 2.43 to 2.44. So 2.87 to 1.74 is not a speedup, and against the faster record runs it is about 1.2 times in both train and eval. Train over eval stays at 0.71 to 0.72 against 0.72 to 0.74, which points to a host gap, though the forward changed too.
The one place the list may flatter the entry is thin rows, because embedding backward is charged per element. On this Mac the time follows the price: 43.7 to 24.0 ns per row, and a table step falls from about 12 to 10 ms, 17% less for 11.4% less in ticks. On the agent's x86 box it may not. Base and v1 took 51.4 and 50.6 s and v3 and v4 50.9 and 49.8, then v5 and v6 took 57.4 and 59.2 at equal concurrency within five minutes, while eval fell from 3.70 to 3.51 ns per tick. Taking 5.8 s for the gate, base and v5 both come to about 19.2 ms per table step. If that holds, the 12.9% more table bytes cost about 12% more wall on x86, a discount from the list and not a saving. The same box later ran this code at anywhere from 71 to 112 s, so this is suggestive only. The work is really done and paid at list, and the effect is far inside the factor of 5 the engine's calibration allows, so I do not call it an exploit. A per-row backward charge would shrink the table part. The gate part, about 0.0023, would remain, just above the 0.002 margin.
The 5.4 to 5.9 gate multiple does not show. On the agent's box g0 and g1 ran head to head at 1.81 and 1.53 ns per tick against about 1.40 for tables, 1.29 and 1.09 times. Under heavier equal contention gw2, this stage set, took 5.06 against claim 1's whole runs at 3.09 to 3.18, about 1.6 times. On this Mac it goes from 0.88 to 1.03 times, and wall per gate step falls 36% for 42% fewer ticks. So the multiple may creep up but stays between 1 and about 1.6, on 8.1% of the ticks.
Seed SD is 0.00052 in both stages against 0.00097, ratios 0.536 and 0.540 against LOW_VARIANCE_RATIO 0.3, so no flag. Init and data order come from the verifier's seed and nothing fixes them.

5. Selection. The gain stands clear. Stage 1 is +0.00412 +- 0.00086, p beyond margin 1.1e-4. Stage 2 is +0.00406 +- 0.00086, p 1.4e-4. The worst of 16 seeds (1.08992) beats the best of the record's 24 (1.09097). Both stages share the record's 8 seeds, which run about 0.0002 above its other 16, so 0.0041 is slightly high. Against all 24 it is +0.00391 +- 0.00045, p 1.6e-10, and against attempt-05's 16 it is +0.00381 +- 0.00053, p 1.4e-7. Null batches of identical code have means with SD 0.00027 to 0.00038 and range at most 0.00105.
Two claims means two tries at alpha 0.01, and doubling gives 2.2e-4 and 2.8e-4, still clear. Claim 1 is not independent evidence, as the two share their table phase exactly. It passed stage 1 and failed stage 2 (+0.00293, p 0.036) on the margin and not on the sign, and its size is what the slope predicts for 353 steps, so it is no warning. The agent's note that claim 2 is not shown better than claim 1 is overtaken: on 16 sealed seeds each, claim 2 is better by 0.00094 +- 0.00046.

Could not check: how the thin-table gain splits among more steps, fewer collisions and narrower rows, which needs full-budget runs; per-phase wall on the verifier's hosts, since the seed logs are empty and verify.json holds totals; whether this entry's verifier host matched attempt-05's; `np.add.at` time on x86. All my timings are this Mac under load, on Accelerate rather than OpenBLAS.
Verdict: real
