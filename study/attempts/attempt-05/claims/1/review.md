# Review of attempt-05, claim 1: the incumbent's model with its gate refitted

Numbers come from verify.json, base.json, the incumbent's test.json and dev.json, attempt-04's verify.json, the agent's transcript and work files, and my own short pricing and causality runs on this Mac. Variant names are the agent's directories under work/. "Seed 11" means its cached tables for that seed, scored on dev.

1. Mechanism. The gain is a better fit of the gate, and not more table training or a new source of compute. The diff against the incumbent touches only ROWS, gate_params, gates, the step count and an AdaGrad branch. Walking real step prices to the budget reproduces train_ticks exactly for both entries (39,982,688,386 and 39,965,835,468), and the incumbent's table phase ends at 36,751,489,472, the figure in the agent's cache log, so these counts are exact.
Table phase: 2,376 steps and 19,464,192 target bytes at 1,888.8 ticks per byte, against 2,372 steps and 19,431,424 at 1,891.3, so 0.14% more bytes per tick. Gate phase: 244 batches, 244 steps and 249,856 bytes at 12,883 ticks per byte, against 130 batches, 390 steps and 133,120 bytes at 24,146, so 1.87 times the bytes per tick and 0.63 times the steps.
The extra bytes come from the single step. Ticks per batch: incumbent 24,725,447; incumbent at one step 12,920,911 (47.7% less); entry 13,191,922; entry with the MLP back 13,327,166; entry without the hash table 12,763,603. Removing the MLP frees 1.0% of a batch and the new table costs 3.4%, so the docstring's "its ticks go to more data" is worth about 1%.
What each part buys, on identical cached tables: this gate (m2) beats `base` by 0.00477 on seed 11 and 0.00420 on seed 12, close to the sealed gain, so the row cut costs nothing visible. The seed 11 walk: AdaGrad plus the hash table, still with three steps and the MLP (kt), 0.00185; no MLP and two steps (m1), 0.00203; one step (m2), 0.00089. The second table is a minor part: 0.00027 (ag to g5agh) or 0.00057 (nag to kn) at 2x512 and three steps, at most 12% of the gain and below the screening noise in point 3. Doubling its rows moved 0.00002 (n4093), and under Adam it costs 0.00136 (g5h).
"Short of fresh bytes, not steps" is half right. On the incumbent's fitter at 2x512, one step (r1) gets 1.66 times the bytes per tick and scores 0.00029 worse than three, and two steps (r2) 0.00063 worse, while twice the budget with both more bytes and more steps gains only 0.00181 (t16). Fewer steps pay only after the tables move to AdaGrad and the MLP goes (kn to m3 0.00091, m1 to m2 0.00089). So about 0.003 of the seed 11 gain comes from the optimizer change making each byte count, which is my inference, not the byte count alone.
The agent's other figures hold in its logs on one seed: four times the budget gains 0.00474 and 0.00477 (t32, two seeds, "about 0.005"); six passes cost 0.00346 (r6); removing the MLP helps 0.00017 to 0.00050. Removing the previous-byte table costs 0.00536 against its parent t512; the quoted 0.0055 is against nomlp, which is not its parent. The agent never ablated the final configuration: there is no m2 without the hash table, with the MLP, or with Adam.

2. Honesty. Clean. `gates` reads x only. The g5 row at t is (x[t] * 251 + x[t-1]) % 2039, and x[t] is the byte before the target. At t = 0 the previous byte is replaced by 0 in both train and eval, so the first bytes stay causal; that position shares a row with a real pair after a zero byte, a small impurity and not a leak. AdaGrad state is not returned, nothing lives at module level, and static_problems is empty.
Twins are exactly 0.0 on all 16 sealed seeds and all 16 of the agent's dev seeds. The incumbent shows 8.56e-6 to 1.05e-5 on test, but that is a host property: attempt-04's sandbox logged only 0.0 while building that file, and among 2-CPU verifier jobs nonzero twins appear only in four, all at 2.64 ns per tick or slower. The features cannot defeat the check. With random parameters at the eval shape, rows moved within the batch, and 12 cuts from 1 to 511, logits before the cut differ by 0.0 and after the cut by at least 10.16 nats, and a repeated call differs by 0.0. A control where g5 reads the next byte moves 2.69 nats before the cut, far past the 1e-4 tolerance.

3. Dev tuning. The transcript has 70 cached-table dev scores over 60 names (59 variants and the incumbent's `base`), 60 of them on seed 11 alone and 10 on seed 12, from 17 table caches, plus two 8-seed full runs (c1, c2). That is 86 dev scores against a `seed_runs_before` of 16, which counts only c1 and c2 because gate.py calls `--child-eval` directly. The agent's 62 is an honest directory count, though "seeds 11 and 12" flatters what was mostly one seed.
Claimed 0.00549 against verified 0.00472 is a shortfall of 0.00076, or 0.00058 against the 16-seed pool of 0.00490. Its standard error is 0.00082, so it cannot be told from zero. The paired screening gain of 0.00420 to 0.00477 is already below the sealed figure, and the claimed gain compares two unpaired dev runs with the incumbent's SD at 0.00158. I put selection at 0 to about 0.0008. Screening noise is real: y20 looked 0.00059 better on two seeds, then lost by 0.00018 on 8 fresh seeds, and the agent reported that and dropped it, which was the right call.
No constant carries held-out information beyond ordinary tuning. GATE_TAB is a coarse pick (0.05, 0.1, 0.15, 0.2, 0.3 tried), 251 and 2039 are the largest primes below 256 and 2048, LOCAL and MIXW are unchanged, re-centred constants from calib.py (train data only) were rejected, and literals total 2,888 of 4,096 bytes. No test window was read: isolation.json shows 0 nonzero bytes across 21,544 test windows, gate.py hard-codes dev, and the 34 tool calls are Bash and Read with no command naming heldout, data/, http or urllib. It made no attempt to rebuild the split from the web, although the prompt allowed the web.

4. Price exploitation. None found. Eval costs 12,171 ticks per byte against 12,180, which is 24.3% of the 50,000 cap. Python lines are 1,452,646 in train and 113,012 in eval, cap 20,000,000. Item reads are 0 on all 16 seeds and absent from the source, with no builtin sum and no long-line trick, so the open holes are unused. Peak memory is 199.2 to 199.9 MB against 199.3 to 199.5. Parameters are 4,176,036 plus 34 name characters, 18,234 under the cap: minus 48,096 for rows, minus 976 for the MLP, plus 36,702 for g5. g5 pays the listed embedding, backward and elementwise prices.
The 58.2 s against 109.0 s of train wall (1.456 against 2.728 ns per tick) is a host gap and not a speedup. The machine strings match, but nearly the same forward takes 15.9 s against 29.6 s, a factor of 1.86, against 1.87 for train; train over eval ns per tick is 0.728 against 0.736. So no cheap compute appears, and the gate multiple did not move much against the incumbent. The 5.4 to 5.9 multiple does not show here. On the agent's x86 box in the same minutes, tables ran 1.43 to 1.44 ns per tick (z06, z11) and the gate about 1.0 to 1.5 (3 s for 2.44e9 ticks, 5 to 6 s for 4.45e9, rounded seconds); at 19:10 kt with three steps and m2 with one both took 5 s. If the verifier host's table rate was 1.47, as in record 07's job, 4.2 s remain for 3.22e9 gate ticks, 1.3 ns per tick, but that host match is an assumption. This Mac under load gives gate 0.69 against 0.70 and tables 0.73 against 0.71. One step does raise the no_grad stage work from 28% to 53% of gate ticks, without visible wall excess.
Seed SD is 0.00099 and 0.00081 against 0.00135, ratios of 0.73 and 0.60 against LOW_VARIANCE_RATIO 0.3, and verify.json carries no flag.

5. Selection. The gain stands clear. Stage 1 is 0.00472 +- 0.00128, p 0.00026 beyond the margin, and stage 2 is 0.00508 +- 0.00122, p 7.7e-5, with lower bounds of 0.00344 and 0.00386 against the 0.002 margin. Both stages share the incumbent's same 8 seeds, which run high: that file (same sha256) scored 1.09704 and 1.09732 on attempt-04's 16 test seeds, against 1.09759 for the seated 8. Against all 24 seeds the gain is 0.00463 +- 0.00061, p 1.4e-10, and that is my best estimate, 2.3 times the margin. The test windows were zeros in the agent's copy, so its screening could not adapt to them, it filed one claim, and the disjoint dev and test windows agree at 0.0042 to 0.0048 and 0.0046.

Could not check: an ablation of the final configuration at the real budget, since it needs a table phase and the training lock, so the split among one step, no MLP, AdaGrad and the hash table rests on single-seed cached runs at mixed shapes, and differences under about 0.0008 are not resolved; per-phase wall on the verifier's hosts or their CPU models, so the gate multiple there is inferred; my twin and causality runs were on this Mac, not x86.

Verdict: real
