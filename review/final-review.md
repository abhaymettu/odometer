# Final review before going public

Hostile read of commit 7d3052b on 2026-11-02. Nothing was trained. "Simulated" means my own short simulation with `stats.welch` and normal seeds. Line numbers are at that commit.

Verdict: do not publish as it stands. The records and the series plan hold up. The headline statistic says little, the novelty sentence is one link from embarrassment, private notes sit in the tree and history, and several public numbers are wrong.

## Findings, worst first

1. **The novelty sentence is one link from a public correction.** README:149 says of a per-operation training budget "What I couldn't find anywhere", then "that's the part to judge this on"; write-up:39 and paper:14,71 repeat it. ARC's White-Box Estimation Challenge (Hilton, Christiano, Wu, announced 2 June 2026, five months before this repo's first commit) has a public AIcrowd leaderboard whose docs say it "ranks on the FLOPs flopscope counted and nothing else", through a metered NumPy wrapper, with a budget of 2^41 per MLP. A participant describes its cost model as 472 op weights, so it is not raw FLOPs either. Its Phase 1 wall-time term was arbitraged by entrants, which is the failure "Why a meter" predicts. It meters estimation on random MLPs, not a training run, so the narrow words survive: in 10 searches I found no public record track that meters the training run itself. The reader will not feel that distinction was disclosed. The other neighbours are also missing: Green AI (2019) proposed operation counts as a hardware-independent measure of work, the MicroNet Challenge (NeurIPS 2019) scored inference on computed math operations, and FLOPpy (2026) reports hardware-agnostic FLOPs for backward and optimizer steps with no budget. LoRA Speedrun (seen only on a Product Hunt mirror) has a referee rerun each record 3 times on fresh seeds, on a fixed GPU, which weakens the differentiator at paper:71. `review/novelty-and-exploits.md`, which README:149 calls "the full check", spent about 4 searches on this against a draft, and its line 79 still offers "the first open, standing record table". Fix: replace the sentence everywhere with "The nearest precedent we know is ARC's White-Box Estimation Challenge (2026), whose public leaderboard ranks estimators on FLOPs counted per operation by a metered NumPy wrapper. It meters estimation, not training. Green AI (2019) proposed operation counts as a hardware-independent measure of work, and the MicroNet Challenge (2019) scored inference that way. We found no public record track that meters the training run itself, from about a dozen searches, which is not proof." Cut "the part to judge this on". Cite ARC in "Why a meter", since it supports the argument. Stop calling the review "the full check", add these sources to it and strike line 79.

2. **7 of 7 was close to automatic, and it still leads.** Dev and test are a random half split of one file (`_data.py:53`), so there is no shift: 764 of the 1024 scored test windows sit next to a dev-pool window in the file, and stories average 800 bytes against 513-byte windows. `replicated` has no margin, and agents can read the 0.002 margin in `verify.py`. The smallest claim was 0.0026, or 6.7 standard errors of the difference. Simulated with each claim's seed SDs: if every claim were exactly true, all 7 replicate with probability above 0.999, and if every true gain were half its claim, still about 0.66. The only claim ever near the noise was pilot-01's 0.0009, inconclusive, on another model. The model and count were filled in right after pilot-02's two claims passed (51075a1 says TBD, 4bd956c reports the pass at 06:52, 5c3343c fills them in at 06:54). A sceptic says the series shows that one model, chosen after a good pilot, finds 0.0025 to 0.0188 bpb in 40 minutes on recipes seated that morning, and reports dev means honestly when dev and test are exchangeable. It does not show agent claims are trustworthy where failure is possible. The docs hedge ("far above seed noise") but never say the count could hardly have come out otherwise, and write-up:27 says selection "has little room to inflate anything" with no number, while the reviews record 42 and 44 variants. Fix: lead with the record rule (5 of 7 claims cleared the margin twice, gains falling from 0.0188 to 0.0041), and put this beside every 7 of 7: "Had every claim been exact, all 7 would replicate with probability above 0.999, and at half the claimed gain about 0.66, so this count mainly rules out a broken pipeline or misreported dev means." 4. **"Sealed" is true only inside the series sandbox.** Paper:14 and README:25 say "sealed split". For everyone else, `prepare.py` downloads the test windows, the permutation seed is public (`_data.py:53`), and `--split` defaults to test (`verify.py:419`). README:79 and SUBMITTING step 1 tell an entrant to run `--smoke` with no split, which reads 32 test windows. That is the same slip README:142, TUNING:312 and the record 08 note in `records/names.json` record as an accident. `--against` then gives test scores as often as anyone likes. README:142 says the static rules "limit what that buys" and gives no number. The literal cap of 4096 bytes is 32,768 bits against 524,288 scored bytes, a ceiling of 0.0625 bpb: 31 times the margin and above the 0.0553 that the 5 series records gained together. A real attack would get a fraction of that, but nobody measured it; the attacks README admits a 4 to 8 KB table "remains possible", and record 11 already uses 3150 literal bytes. Fix: say "sealed" only about the series. State that the test split is public to entrants, so a new record rests on published logs and review. Change README and SUBMITTING to `--smoke --split dev` (`verify.py` cannot change without breaking `code_sha256`). Add an attack entry that spends the whole literal budget on text chosen from test windows, measure its gain, and put that number in README:142.

5. **The record rule's power figure is wrong in four public places.** README:145, write-up:37, paper:74 and copy.json:7 say the record rule passes a true gain of one seed SD 31% of the time. That figure comes from review item 4, a plain Welch test with no margin and one stage, which is `replicated` (I get 28% with Welch's own df and 30% with df 14). Simulated for the actual rule at seed SD 0.001, with margin and both stages: 0% at a gain of 0.001, 0.1% at 0.002, 15% at 0.003, 48% at 0.0035, 83% at 0.004, 99.8% at 0.005. So the docs misstate the rule by orders of magnitude at the very size they discuss, and the real weakness, that a true gain of 1.5 times the margin usually fails, goes unsaid. Attempt-06 claim 1 is the example: gain 0.0034, stage 2 p = 0.036. Fix: "At a seed SD of 0.001 the record rule passes a true gain of 0.003 about 15% of the time and 0.004 about 83%. The weaker `replicated` test passes one seed SD about 30% of the time."

12. **The hardware sentence is flat and over-general.** README:11 and write-up:5 say "the same score", and README:62 and paper:20 say the reading is a pure function of shapes or of the program.
